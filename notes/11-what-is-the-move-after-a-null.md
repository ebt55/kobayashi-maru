# 11 — 16:00 IST — what is the move after a null? (and the incident model joins)

Ebin asked the right question: are we getting expected results, or is the data too thin to say; should we change the
measurement or the APIs to get "better" results?

My answer, and why. The primary result is not thin. Six hundred solvable item-runs, zero events, across five levels.
The pre-registered null criterion was "flat within ±5 points"; we are inside ±1. The predictions I wrote down (P1, P2)
are wrong for this model, and the plan said in advance that this outcome is informative. So the move is not to change
the instrument. A changed DV, a changed rule, a "more tempting" environment chosen after seeing the null — that is the
garden of forking paths, and a careful judge would find it. What is legitimate: run the arms the plan already named
that test *different mechanisms*, label them secondary, and keep the primary conclusion untouched. Those are the peer
arm (the incident's second ingredient), more lineages (running), the incident model itself (Sol, approved), and the
plan's optional arm B′ — one continuous session per batch — which I now think is the most important of the four,
because the per-item design gives the agent a deliberately thin memory (a manifest and its own summaries), and the
incident's agents had long continuous trajectories. If pressure travels between items anywhere, it travels there.
I wrote P7 for it (≥ 1 solvable-item cheat at f = 0.60 in the continuous arm) at 35% and committed it before the arm
ran (PREREG v5).

Bias I am watching. I want P7 to come out true, because "spillover needs a continuous context" is a tidier story than
"no spillover". The prediction is on record at 35%, so a positive result will be read against that, and a negative one
stands as a second null, not as a reason to try a third memory channel.

Sol. Its rules-v2 pilot: 0/15 cheats on impossible items in the standard environment, 10/10 honest on solvable.
That makes every hosted line except Haiku a ≤ 1/15 line under the strict rule. The frontier model of the incident does
not touch a hidden grader when told not to, on toy tasks, even when the task is impossible. Salient it goes. At $0.02
per item (cache reads dominate) the cost rule gave it the full five-level grid — a full curve for the model that was in
the swarm, for about $19. I had budgeted $55 for endpoints only; my token estimate from the plan was five times too
high because I did not model cache reads.
